# Intake question bank

Ask in order, but follow the conversation. The reason column is there so the asker can explain any question that gets pushback. An intake is a conversation with a busy person, not a form.

## If you only get five questions

Ranked. The first is the anchor. The rest catch the failures the anchor misses.

1. **"What does this person have to deliver in the first twelve months for you to call it a great hire?"** The answer becomes the brief, the scorecard, the pitch and the reference-check frame.
2. **"Walk me through how your last five deals got done."** Shows whether a repeatable motion exists and what kind of seller can run it. Essential for any first or early sales hire.
3. **"Who else has to say yes, and do they agree with what you just told me?"** Finds the hidden veto before the first shortlist.
4. **"Why is this role open now, and what happens if it is still empty in six months?"** Tests urgency, budget and whether everyone agrees on the purpose.
5. **"What are you afraid of with this hire?"** Surfaces the last mis-hire the team will over-correct for.

## The seven sections

### 1. Business context

| Question | Why ask it |
|---|---|
| What is driving this hire now? | The real driver (a funding round, a lost deal, a board target) sets the urgency and the pitch |
| New headcount or a backfill? | A backfill inherits whatever went wrong last time |
| If a backfill, why did the last person leave? | Two people leaving the same seat inside a year points at the role or the manager, not the candidate pool |
| What stage is the business at, in numbers? | Revenue, customers, team size and runway decide which candidates will take the risk |
| Why would someone good join now? | If nobody inside can answer, no candidate will either |

### 2. The role

| Question | Why ask it |
|---|---|
| Where does 80% of the first ninety days go? | Separates the real job from the title |
| How hands-on versus strategic is it? | A senior title with hands-on scope is a common mismatch |
| What do most candidates misunderstand about this role? | Becomes a screening question and a line in the pitch |
| What must they have versus what would be nice? | Feeds the three must-haves |
| What is missing in the team today? | The hire is often filling a gap nobody named |

### 3. Reporting and stakeholders

| Question | Why ask it |
|---|---|
| Who do they report to, and how does that person manage? | Candidates choose managers as much as companies |
| Which relationships make or break this role? | Tells the panel who should interview |
| Who else signs off the hire? | Every veto holder must agree the brief before sourcing starts |

### 4. The person

| Question | Why ask it |
|---|---|
| Which behaviours are non-negotiable? | Behaviours predict better than credentials |
| Who has not worked out here, and why? | The fastest route to the real deal-breakers |
| What would prove early that someone can deliver? | Becomes the work sample or the first-interview test |
| What red flags should be caught before a CV is sent? | Saves the hiring manager's time and the recruiter's credibility |

### 5. Success

| Question | Why ask it |
|---|---|
| What are the twelve-month outcomes, in numbers? | Ask again if the anchor question got skills instead of outcomes |
| What does a strong hire look like at day ninety? | Separates ramp from failure |

### 6. The process

| Question | Why ask it |
|---|---|
| What are the stages, and who owns each? | Unowned stages are where candidates stall |
| How fast will you give feedback at each stage? | Sets the feedback contract (see `process-contract.md`) |
| What is the start date, and is it real? | Notice periods for senior GTM roles often run one to three months |
| How flexible are you for an exceptional candidate? | Pay, title and location are where flexibility usually lives |

### 7. Risk and culture

| Question | Why ask it |
|---|---|
| Any internal politics or sensitivities? | Internal candidates and unhappy peers sink outside hires |
| Any past friction hiring this role? | Patterns repeat |
| What have I not asked that I should have? | The most useful answer often comes last |

## Answers that signal a failing search

Ranked by how often they decide the outcome. When you hear one, stop and fix it before writing the brief.

1. **The team disagrees on why the role exists, or the real decider is not in the room.** Different stakeholders will score against different briefs, and the late veto kills offers.
2. **"We need someone to come in and build the playbook."** In a first sales hire this usually means the founder has not yet sold repeatably, and the hire will be blamed for a problem the company has not solved.
3. **The wish list.** Enterprise and SMB, hunter and farmer, our industry, our stage, five years, all mandatory. Each one shrinks the pool sharply.
4. **"I'll know them when I see them."** No outcomes, only a job description. Every interviewer will invent their own.
5. **"We want someone from a famous company."** A brief built around a logo, not the selling motion this seat runs.

## What first-time founders get wrong

A founder hiring into a function for the first time does not yet know what great looks like. Expect these, and handle them kindly.

1. **Hiring before they have done the job.** A first rep cannot repeat a process that lives only in the founder's head. Ask for the last five deals before briefing a seller.
2. **Wanting a VP when they need someone who sells.** A leader built for scale waits for a machine that does not exist yet. At an early stage the realistic hire is a hands-on seller or a hands-on head of sales.
3. **Buying the logo, not the motion.** Sellers from large brands are used to inbound demand, a known product and a support team. Brief for someone who has built pipeline from nothing.
4. **Not knowing what great looks like, so the brief becomes a wish list.** Suggest two or three short calls with people who are excellent in this role before the brief is final.
5. **Pay, quota and ramp that don't match the market or the sales cycle.** Pay below market only attracts the people the market passed over. Write the ramp down.

## Adapting by seat

| Seat | Add to the intake |
|---|---|
| SDR or BDR | Meetings or pipeline target, who they hand to, how long until promotion is realistic |
| Account executive | Average deal size, cycle length, self-sourced share of pipeline expected, quota as a multiple of OTE |
| Customer success or account management | Book size, renewal and expansion ownership, whether there is any commercial target |
| Marketing | Which job comes first: pipeline, positioning or brand. One person rarely does all three well |
| RevOps or GTM engineering | Systems they inherit, who they serve, build versus maintain split |
| Sales leadership | Is there a repeatable motion to scale yet, team size on day one versus month twelve, how much they still sell personally |
