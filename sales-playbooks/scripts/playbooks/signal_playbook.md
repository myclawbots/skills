# Signal-Based Outreach

The biggest lever on reply rate is relevance at the moment of need. Emails that reference a real,
current signal get 5 to 18 % replies against 1 to 3 % for generic ICP-matched outreach. Signals are
recorded with `pipeline.py observe` and listed with `pipeline.py signals`; this playbook turns one
into a first line.

## The core rule

The signal is the reason you are emailing today. Say it plainly, in the first sentence, as an
observation. Never dress it up ("so impressed to see…"). Never claim more than the signal proves. If the
only signal is "they exist and match the ICP", you have no signal: fall back to a role or industry
problem stated honestly.

Every claim traces to a recorded observation or a stored fact. No invented funding rounds, no "saw your
post" unless an observation says so. A wrong specific is worse than a right generic.

## Signal → angle

**HIRING_ROLE** (they're hiring for a relevant role). The strongest signal: they're buying in your
category this quarter. Angle: the workload behind the hire, not the hire.
Good: "Saw you're hiring a second SDR. Usually means the first one is buried. How are you handling
reply volume while the seat's open?" Bad: "Congrats on the new role posting!"
Only name the role the observation names.

**TECH_STACK / NO_ONLINE_BOOKING / NO_SCHEMA_MARKUP** (something concrete on their site). Angle: a
gap next to what they run, or fitting into it. Good: "Noticed there's no booking widget on your
site. How do after-hours enquiries reach you?" Bad: listing their whole stack back at them. One tool
at most, and only if it carries the point.

**INCUMBENT_AGENCY** (a footer credit names who built or runs the site). Angle: results, not the
incumbent. Never disparage them by name.

**RUNNING_GOOGLE_ADS / RUNNING_META_ADS** (spending on acquisition today). Angle: what happens to
the clicks after they land. A budget signal: they can afford to fix leaks.

**BLOG_STALE** (content started and abandoned). Angle: the cost of the gap, one line, no scolding.

**REVIEW_RATING / REVIEW_COUNT** (strong reviews, weak site). Angle: the mismatch between reputation
and conversion.

**Funding** (recorded as an observation with a source). 60 to 90 days post-raise is peak buying, but
everyone emails then, so be more specific: the pressure the raise creates (doubled pipeline target,
hiring fast), never "congrats on the round".

**New business / new domain.** Only if you sell to early-stage companies. Keep it low-key.

## Which signal wins

Recency and actionability: **hiring > funding > tech gap > tech present > industry problem > nothing**.
Use exactly one signal as the hook. Stacking two reads as a dossier. Bank the rest for email 2.

## Following up on a signal

Email 2 takes a different signal or a proof point, never email 1's hook. If a signal has aged (a job
post now filled), don't present it as current; re-observe or drop it.

## When you have no real signal

Say something true about the role or industry right now: "Most teams at your headcount still reconcile
this by hand." Not "I was looking at your website and love what you're doing."
