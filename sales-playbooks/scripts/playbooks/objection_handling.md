# Objection Handling

The goal is never to "overcome" an objection. It is to understand the concern and redirect toward
value, or to recognise that it is not a concern at all and stop.

## LAARC

**Listen → Acknowledge → Assess → Respond → Confirm**

1. Read the full reply. Find the actual objection, not the surface words.
2. Show you heard them: "That makes sense." "Appreciate you being direct."
3. If it's vague, ask one clarifying question.
4. Address it with evidence from `sales/product_knowledge.md`, a reframe, or flexibility the owner has approved.
5. Check it's resolved, with one question.

## Diagnose before you respond

- **Reflex brush-off** ("not interested", sent within a minute): they evaluated nothing. Don't rebut content they didn't read. Close gracefully, or offer one specific piece of value with no ask.
- **Condition** ("under contract until March"): a fact, not an objection. Log it, ask permission to reconnect at the real date, and stop until then.
- **Smokescreen** ("too expensive" from someone who never asked the price): the real issue is priority or trust. Answer the hidden one: "Fair. If it were free, would it be worth your time?"
- **Genuine objection** (specific, engaged, references their situation): the only kind worth a substantive answer. It's a buying signal.

Reply length is the tell: long reply, respond substantively; one-liner, reply even shorter than they did.

## The four categories

**Budget** ("too expensive", "no budget"). Reframe around the cost of inaction, only with a real
proof point: "{{client}} found it paid for itself in {{timeframe}} through {{specific saving}}." No
proof point in product_knowledge.md: make the argument without one. "We have flexible options, worth
a quick call to see if there's a fit?"

**Authority** ("not the right person", "need to check with my boss"). Ask for the name, or offer to
make the case for them: "Would it help if I put together a one-pager you could share with
{{decision maker title}}?"

**Need** ("already have a solution", "not a priority"). Differentiate or plant a seed: "What's the one
thing you wish your current solution did better?" "When it does become a priority, what would need to
change?"

**Timing** ("not now", "next quarter"). Respect it and stay in orbit: "When would be a good time to
reconnect?" "Mind if I send a short case study in the meantime?" Then actually wait.

## Techniques

- **Isolate and reverse:** "If we could solve {{concern}}, would you be open to exploring this?" Reveals brush-off vs real.
- **Feel, felt, found:** only with a real, named-in-notes example.
- **Cost of inaction:** only when they have shared a problem. Inventing a consequence is a scare tactic.
- **The takeaway:** lukewarm after two exchanges? "Honestly, this doesn't sound like a burning problem for you, and that's fine. Should I close the file?" Hedgers re-engage; non-fits confirm. Both are wins.

## Rules

1. Never argue with an objection.
2. One response per objection. If they push back again, respect it.
3. "Not interested" is final: thank them, close.
4. End every objection response with one question or soft next step.
5. Match their energy and formality.
6. Not a fit? Say so.

## Hard guardrails (override every technique)

1. **Opt-outs are not objections.** "Unsubscribe", "remove me", "stop", "take me off your list", any wording: no pitch, no question, no "before you go". `pipeline.py suppress`, a one-line confirmation at most, never again.
2. **Never fabricate** customers, numbers, deadlines, "two slots left".
3. **Honest about automation** the moment they ask.
4. **Never impersonate**: no claimed prior conversation, referral or role that isn't in the config.
5. **Anger means stop.** No rebuttal, no clever reframe. Brief apology if warranted, suppress, move on.
6. **Legal or compliance objections end the thread.** "This is spam", "how did you get my email", "I'm reporting this": do not reply yourself. Suppress, and hand the thread to the owner with the facts (data source, removal done).
